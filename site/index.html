Oregon Farmland Solar

When an Oregon county approves a solar project on farmland, it writes down why. This site shows the reasons counties gave for approvals, and walks through three cases that were appealed to the Land Use Board of Appeals.

Results are not pooled, and they are not “final” outcomes. County records and LUBA appeal examples are separate source types. They are counted separately and never added together.

County records are approvals only. Denied projects are not in our records, so this site cannot show why projects were denied or how often. We have not checked whether any approval was later appealed.

How appeals work

Three of the cases here were appealed. This is what that means.

How appeals to LUBA work

  1. The county decides.

    County planning staff, a hearings officer or the board of commissioners approves or denies the permit and writes down its reasons (“findings”). If the county has its own appeal step, such as from a hearings officer to the board, that has to finish before anyone can go to LUBA.

  2. Someone appeals.

    The applicant, or someone who opposes the decision, can appeal to the Land Use Board of Appeals (LUBA). The appeal must be filed within 21 days after the decision becomes final, which is generally when it is put in writing. The person appealing is the petitioner. The county is the respondent.

  3. LUBA reviews the record.

    LUBA does not take new evidence. It reads the record the county had, hears legal argument, and checks the decision against land use law. It cannot overturn a decision just because it disagrees with it.

  4. LUBA rules.

    It can affirm (uphold the decision), reverse (overrule it: used when the decision is unlawful and the law prohibits it) or remand (send it back to the county for more work, for example when the findings are not enough or the evidence does not support them). A remand is not the end: the county can decide again. LUBA can also dismiss an appeal, for example if it was filed late.

  5. Further appeal.

    LUBA’s decision can be appealed to the Oregon Court of Appeals within 21 days after LUBA mails its order. We do not track that step.

This is a plain-language summary, not legal advice. Check the deadlines with LUBA before relying on them. The three appeal examples on this site show these steps on real cases.

Words used here

County records
Approved solar projects on farmland, from the state’s list and county documents. Approvals only.
Appeal examples
Three cases taken to LUBA, shown on their own to explain how appeals work. They are not a sample of the approvals.
Petitioner
The person or group appealing to LUBA. It can be the applicant (after a denial) or someone opposed (after an approval).
Reason given
A ground a decision-maker stated for its decision, copied from the document as a direct quote.
Hand-checked
A person read the source and confirmed the reason and its category.
Machine-extracted
Software pulled the reason from the document. A person has not confirmed it yet.
County outcome
What the county decided at that proceeding.
Appeal outcome (where in our records)
What LUBA decided, if that decision is in our records. “Not in our records” does not mean there was no appeal.

Method and credits

Data sources

  • County records: a spreadsheet and about 200 county approval documents (PDFs) provided by the State of Oregon. Approvals only. Not loaded yet.
  • LUBA appeal examples: three LUBA opinions chosen to show how an appeal works.

The two source types are counted separately and are never combined.

What we assume, and have not checked

  • We understand the approvals are completed, with no appeal pending. We have not verified this. An appeal does not automatically stop an approved project, so an appeal does not mean a project stopped.
  • We do not know yet whether a county approval can have more than one reason. Counts are of reasons, not of approvals.
  • One appeal example (a conditional approval) may also be in the county records. We keep them separate.
  • We have not checked which approvals were appealed. “Appeal outcome: Not in our records” means exactly that.

What is not here

Denied projects, except the two county denials inside the appeal examples. The state does not hold that data, so this site cannot say why projects were denied or how often.

County boundaries

Boundaries come from the Bureau of Land Management (BLM). They are generalized for display and are not a legal boundary.

How reasons are coded

The reasons in the appeal examples were read from the opinions and coded by a person. For the county approvals, a program run on our own computer will read each document, pull out each reason with a direct quote from the text, and propose one of eight categories. Every quote is checked to appear word for word on its page. A person checks some of them. Every reason shows whether it was hand-checked or machine-extracted.

Limits

  • Outcomes are shown as of the time we collected them.
  • Synthetic demo data exists only to show the layout.
