## LUBA terms used in this project
Words are used here the way LUBA uses them, which depends on whose argument is being judged.
- **Assignment of error (and subassignment):** a petitioner's argument that the decision under review is wrong for a stated reason.
- **Sustained:** LUBA agrees with the petitioner, so the error is valid. A valid error leads to reversal or remand unless the decision has another sufficient basis (see the last point).
- **Denied:** LUBA rejects the petitioner's argument, so the decision stands on that point. It does not count toward reversal or remand.
- **Upheld (this project's word):** a ground of the decision that LUBA reviewed and did not disturb. Use "upheld" for the decision-maker's ground and keep "sustained" and "denied" for the petitioner's assignments, because the same event reads opposite ways depending on who petitioned. When the applicant petitions against a denial (Yamhill), a denied assignment means the county's ground was upheld. When an opponent petitions against an approval (Jackson), a sustained assignment means the county's ground was rejected.
- **Valid is not the same as decisive.** LUBA can find an error valid and still affirm, if another independent ground supports the result. In Yamhill, LUBA found no evidence for the first Goal 2 finding and affirmed anyway, because the second and third findings were alternative bases.
- **Not reached:** LUBA does not rule on an assignment because another one already decides the outcome. These are untested, not upheld.

### Initial data model and data-handling rules
- **Decision or analysis:** the data model, reason taxonomy, quote checking and publication policy for the first version.
- **Why (and alternatives rejected):**
  - *Model is project -> proceeding -> document -> reason.* One project passes through several proceedings (staff report, county decision, LUBA appeal, court remand), and a reason belongs to a specific document.
  - *Outcomes are split.* `local_outcome` records what a county decided; `appeal_outcome` records what a reviewing body did. A single "outcome" field was rejected because an appeal result of "affirmed" can mean a denial or an approval stands, so pooling them would mislead. Results are reported per source type (luba / county / efsc), never pooled: LUBA covers appeals only and EFSC covers large projects only.
  - *Taxonomy is a versioned data file* (`data/taxonomy/reasons.yaml`, v1 draft from three sample projects), so categories can be added without schema changes. Ids are never renamed or reused; `other_uncoded` plus a note catches gaps and is reviewed periodically for new categories.
  - *Every coded reason carries a verbatim quote and a PDF page.* The quote is checked against that page's text to catch misquotes and model hallucinations. Fuzzy matches are flagged for a person to review.
  - *Source PDFs stay local.* Published: structured data, quotes with page references, and a manifest with checksums. Republishing the PDFs was rejected because public-records documents can contain personal information. CI therefore verifies synthetic data only.
  - *Synthetic demo data is flagged on every record,* is never mixed with real records, and must be labeled in any UI.
  - *The personal-information scan finds candidates only;* a person reviews quotes before they are published.
- **Inputs:** seven sample PDFs held locally (three projects plus two background documents), to be listed in `docs/data-sources.md`.
- **Assumptions and definitions:** farmland is recorded as the decision itself states it (exclusive farm use or high-value farmland), not re-derived. Dual-use means the decision text mentions grazing, crops or pollinator habitat under or between panels.
- **Limitations / open questions:** the taxonomy is a draft from three projects. Tax-lot matching and parcel-level locations are deferred in favor of a county-level map, and the terms of use for tax lot data are unconfirmed. Quote checking relies on locally extracted text and tolerates ordinary OCR noise but not heavy corruption.

### A reason belongs to the decision it explains, not the document that quotes it
- **Decision or analysis:** `Reason.proceeding_id` is the proceeding whose outcome the reason explains. A reason for the county's decision stays under the local proceeding even when the only source is a LUBA opinion quoting the county's findings; LUBA's own reasons for affirming, reversing or remanding stay under the appeal proceeding. This supersedes the convention used in the Marion and Yamhill entries above.
- **Why (and alternatives rejected):** the dashboard counts the county's grounds for approval or denial, and in the sample set those grounds are available only as LUBA quotes them. Attaching them to the appeal would drop them from county-level counts. The alternative (a second field marking "reasons for the local decision") was rejected as redundant: the proceeding and the document already say which decision a reason explains and where the text was found.
- **Assumptions and definitions:** an appeal document may supply reasons for the local proceeding of the same project; no other cross-proceeding link is allowed (a county document cannot explain an appeal, and nothing crosses projects). `validate` enforces this.
- **Limitations / open questions:** a reason quoted second-hand by LUBA is LUBA's characterization of the county's finding, so the note on each such reason says so. Where the county's own decision is later obtained, its reasons should be coded from that document and the LUBA-quoted duplicates removed.

### Scope: report what the records show, not final outcomes
- **Decision or analysis:** the project reports reasons found in the decisions we hold. It does not claim to report final outcomes or to cover all solar-on-farmland permits in Oregon. Two result labels are used everywhere: "county outcome" (headline) and "appeal outcome (where in our records)".
- **Why (and alternatives rejected):** the records request covers county decisions, and the LUBA opinions were collected by hand, so appeals and court decisions are incomplete. A county approval that LUBA later reversed, or a denial remanded and later approved, would be mislabeled by a "final outcome" field. Collecting the missing appeals and court decisions was rejected for the sprint: with 200+ documents expected, it cannot be done in the time available. A later targeted lookup of LUBA opinions for appealed projects is possible follow-up work.
- **Assumptions and definitions:** the county outcome is the outcome of the local proceeding; the appeal outcome is the outcome of the appeal proceeding in our records, if any. Remanded appeals are unresolved. Where the records request shows a decision was appealed but we hold no appeal document, the project is shown with no appeal outcome, not as final.
- **Limitations / open questions:** selection bias is likely (LUBA samples are appealed cases; the records request may over- or under-represent approvals). Check what the request returns when the files arrive, including any field recording that a decision was appealed.

### Coding rule for LUBA-sourced reasons: only grounds LUBA reviewed and upheld
- **Decision or analysis:** when the source is a LUBA opinion, code only the county grounds that LUBA reviewed and upheld. Where LUBA affirmed on one sufficient basis and did not rule on the county's other grounds, the unreviewed grounds are not coded; this entry records that they exist.
- **Why (and alternatives rejected):** LUBA affirms a denial if at least one ground survives and does not rule on the rest (it would be an advisory opinion). A ground LUBA never reached is untested, not validated, so coding it alongside upheld grounds would suggest it carried the same weight. Coding every county ground, as will be done for un-appealed county decisions, was rejected for the LUBA cases to keep them to what actually decided the outcome. A per-reason "upheld / not reached" field was rejected as unnecessary under this rule.
- **Applied to:** Yamhill Creek Solar. The county denied on six approval standards (OAR 660-033-0130(38)(f)(B) and (C); YCZO 1202.02(B), 1202.02(D), 1202.02(F); YCZO 1101.02(A)(4)). LUBA reviewed only YCZO 1202.02(B) (consistency with comprehensive plan goals and policies, weighed together) and upheld it, so the grounds coded are the findings under that standard. The soil-compaction reason (yamhill-r2) was swapped from the state-standard finding, which LUBA did not review, to the county's finding under the plan's soil-resources goal, which LUBA upheld. The summary "On balance" conclusion restates the individual findings and is not coded separately (see the one-reason-per-ground rule below).
- **Coding rule (all sources):** one reason per distinct ground; a summary or conclusion is coded only if it adds a ground the details do not.
- **Limitations / open questions:** the LUBA cases are coded more narrowly than un-appealed county decisions will be, so they are reported separately and never pooled. Yamhill r1 was swapped from the overall "on balance" conclusion to the separate "can be located on non-farmland" ground in the same final-weighing section.

### Scope update: county records are approvals only (confirmed by the agency)
- **Decision or analysis:** the county-level data is treated as approved decisions only. This replaces the earlier assumption (unconfirmed) in the scope entry above. The README scope statement is updated to match.
- **Why (and alternatives rejected):** the agency's reply to the records request states that the dataset does not include denied or withdrawn applications, and that the PDFs are for approved decisions only. The alternative, presenting the county records as a mix of outcomes, was rejected because the records contain no denials by construction (selection on outcome).
- **Inputs:** the agency's reply to the records request (held locally; not in the repository).
- **Assumptions and definitions:** "approved" includes decisions that may have been appealed or remanded before approval; the history before approval is not in the records. The agency's dataset was built from the PDFs, so the spreadsheet and PDFs describe the same decisions. County records have no local decision numbers, so rows cannot be tied to a specific PDF by number.
- **Limitations / open questions:** denial reasoning exists in the data only for the two LUBA sample cases that are county denials (Marion and Yamhill; the third, Jackson, is a county approval LUBA reversed), so no comparison of approvals and denials is possible and no approval rates can be computed. The reason taxonomy was drafted from denial grounds and will need a stance (supports approval, supports denial, condition) and approval-oriented categories once approval documents or the spreadsheet columns are seen. The records request is a single request: the agency releases nothing until it is complete and paid, but will send the spreadsheet immediately if the PDF portion is withdrawn.

### Synthetic demo records shaped like the expected county approvals
- **Decision or analysis:** a seeded generator (`ofs synth`, `src/oregon_farmland_solar/synthetic.py`) writes 60 invented projects to `data/synthetic/synthetic-county-approvals.json`. Each has one county approval (approved or approved with conditions), one county decision document and one to three reasons. The generator uses seed 20261022.
- **Why (and alternatives rejected):** the real county records are expected after the demo, and the three real sample cases are two county denials and one reversed approval, which is too few for a county map or dashboard. The synthetic file is shaped like the approvals-only records the agency described so the front end can be built against realistic structure. Making the synthetic data resemble the three LUBA cases was rejected because those are appeals, which the county records will not contain. Generating denials was rejected because the county records contain none.
- **Assumptions and definitions:** every record has `synthetic: true` and every quote starts with `[SYNTHETIC]`; project names begin with "Synthetic". Counties are the 36 real Oregon counties so the map has real geometry to draw on, but the project-to-county assignment is random. Reason categories are limited to the five existing categories that can describe an approval (state standards, goal exception, neighbor impacts, visual or tourism, farmland loss). No document has a checksum or file name, and quotes are not checked against any PDF. The generator is deterministic, and a test checks that the committed file equals the generator's output for the default seed and count.
- **Limitations / open questions:** the distributions (outcomes, categories, acreage, counties) are arbitrary and carry no information about real decisions, so no chart built from them may be read as a finding. The five categories are denial-era labels reused for approvals; a stance field is expected once real approval documents are seen (see the scope update above).

### County boundary layer from the BLM feature service
- **Decision or analysis:** the map uses county polygons only (no statewide tax lots). `uv run ofs counties` downloads the BLM county boundaries layer from the Oregon GeoHub's feature service, keeps the 36 Oregon counties and writes `data/geo/oregon-counties.geojson` plus a provenance file.
- **Why (and alternatives rejected):** the layer is an official Oregon source with a query service that returns GeoJSON, so a script can fetch it without the Hub download page (whose link was dead). The Census cartographic boundary files have FIPS codes built in, but are not an Oregon source; they remain a fallback. Storing the original as a release asset was rejected because the script can re-fetch it and the checksum is recorded.
- **Assumptions and definitions:** the original response is saved unmodified in `data/raw/geo/` (git-ignored) and never edited. Derived properties are `fips` ("41" plus the three digits of the BLM code), `name` (matched to our county list) and `cobcode` (unchanged). Geometry in the full-resolution file is unchanged; the committed file is a simplified copy (see limitations). The server reprojects from NAD83 to WGS84 longitude/latitude for GeoJSON; the shift is small for display and is recorded in the provenance steps.
- **Limitations / open questions:** the layer covers Washington too, and six county names occur in both states, so the filter uses the `OR`/`WA` code prefix, never the name; tests require exactly 36 counties with unique FIPS codes and name/code agreement. The layer is editable and BLM may update it without notice, so the access date and checksum identify the version used. The boundaries are not legal boundaries or surveys. The full-resolution layer was 8.5 MB (about 232,000 vertices), too heavy for a web page, so the committed file is simplified to 5% of vertices with mapshaper 0.7.92 (shared borders simplified together, keep-shapes, coordinate precision 0.0001 degrees), about 240 KB. A side-by-side render of full, 10% and 5% looked the same at state scale; the setting is a parameter and is recorded in the provenance file. The full-resolution file is rebuilt locally by `ofs counties` and is not committed. Simplified boundaries are for display only, not measurement or legal use.

### Site data layer: what the website counts and how
- **Decision or analysis:** `ofs site-data` turns the records into `site/data/site-data.json`: per-county tallies keyed by FIPS code, category counts, flat project rows and, for real data only, a case file per project (proceedings in order, each with its reasons, quotes, page numbers and documents).
- **Why (and alternatives rejected):** the site is static, so all aggregation is done ahead of time in Python where it can be tested, and the page only draws. Computing counts in the browser was rejected because the logic would be untested and could differ from what the repo reports.
- **Assumptions and definitions:**
  - Real and synthetic records are separate top-level sections and are never added together. Inside a section, results are grouped by source type (luba, county, ...) and never pooled across types.
  - "County outcome" is the outcome of the project's local proceeding (the latest, if there are several). "Appeal outcome (where in our records)" is the outcome of the most recent appeal by decision date; the number of appeals is kept alongside it, and the case file lists every appeal. A project with no appeal in our records is counted as "none", not as final.
  - Category counts are counts of reasons, not projects, and are split by whether the reason sits under a local or an appeal proceeding. Every active taxonomy category appears, even with a count of zero.
  - Project counties are joined to the county layer by FIPS code through the Oregon county table; an unknown county stops the build.
  - The required result labels ("county outcome", "appeal outcome (where in our records)") and the synthetic notice are included in the data (`meta`), so the site cannot show a result without them.
- **Safeguards:** the build runs the dataset checks and the personal-information scan, refuses a real dataset that contains a synthetic record or the reverse, and a test requires the committed file to equal a fresh build from the committed records.
- **Limitations / open questions:** with all real records coming from LUBA cases, the real section has three projects and says nothing about approval or denial rates. A project with several appeals shows only the latest appeal's outcome in the row. The synthetic section shows approvals only, matching the expected county records. Notes on reasons are included in case files; whether to show them on the site is a design decision.

### Local-model extraction pipeline (`ofs extract`)
- **Decision or analysis:** a local language model (Ollama, `qwen3:30b-instruct`, Apache 2.0) reads one PDF page at a time and proposes quotes that state a decision-maker's grounds. Output is candidates only: uncoded (`other_uncoded`), `hand_checked: false`, labeled with the model's guess at source (county or LUBA).
- **Why (and alternatives rejected):** the full county set is about 200 PDFs, too many to read by hand first. A cloud model was rejected because documents may hold personal information. The plain `qwen3:30b` tag was rejected: it is thinking-only and its reasoning leaked into the output. Free-text summaries were dropped from the output after trials showed they overclaimed (for example calling an opponent's comment a county finding).
- **Prompt trials (page 23 of LUBA 18009, and page 1 of a LUBA opinion):** the first prompt returned the disposition line ("AFFIRMED 09/21/2023") as a reason. A later version invented a quote on a page with no reasons, so asking the model to return an empty list was not reliable by itself. Version 1 adds a `has_reasons` yes/no field first, excludes testimony the decision-maker only reports, and forbids words not on the page. With it, page 1 gave no reasons and page 23 gave three reasons, repeated across five runs. This is two pages, not a validation.
- **Safeguards:** (1) the quote gate: a quote must appear word for word on the page (after normalizing case, punctuation and margin line numbers); a fuzzy-only match goes to "needs review"; quotes under 6 or over 40 words, mostly capitals, duplicated or not on the page are rejected and logged. A replay of the observed model outputs against the real pages accepted the three real reasons and rejected the invented quote and the disposition line. (2) Malformed model output is recorded as a page error, never repaired by guessing. (3) Local server only; cloud models need a typed confirmation or an explicit flag. (4) Temperature 0 and a fixed seed are sent on every request and recorded. (5) Reason ids are assigned after sorting by page and position, so they are stable across runs.
- **Audit trail:** each run writes a manifest (pipeline commit, prompt checksum, model name and digest, settings, input PDF checksum, counts, output checksum). Outputs are kept local and git-ignored until a person has reviewed them; the committed records remain the hand-checked ones.
- **Limitations / open questions:** the gate proves a quote exists on the page, not that it is a reason, that the category or source label is right, or that LUBA upheld it (the "code only grounds LUBA reviewed and upheld" rule needs document context or a human). Page-level calls lose context across page breaks. Accuracy against the hand-coded cases has not been measured yet; a per-page comparison report is the next step. Results were observed on one Mac (M5 Pro, 64 GB) and are not guaranteed identical elsewhere.

### Design handoff (Claude Design)
- **Decision or analysis:** the site is built to a design reference made in Claude Design: style guide, accessibility notes, contrast table, W3C-format tokens, and a build brief (`design/`). `site/css/site-tokens.css` and `site/css/site.css` are copied unchanged from `design/assets/`. The mockup previews (placeholder numbers and invented quotes) are not committed.
- **Why:** a written design reference gives the build a fixed target and keeps colors in one place (tokens only, no hard-coded values).
- **Safeguards:** `tests/test_design_tokens.py` recomputes the WCAG contrast of every text, edge and focus pairing in both light and dark from the CSS itself (it does not trust `contrast.md`), checks that the ramp gets stronger as counts rise, that the site CSS equals the design copy, that the DTCG file uses the same colors, and that the stylesheets load nothing from other servers.
- **Where the design and our data differ** (to settle while building; the data wins): (1) the build brief says the appeal examples' county decision is an approval, but two of the three are county denials (Marion, Yamhill); the design's style guide already says so. (2) The design shows appeal reasons as petitioner assignments of error with LUBA's ruling. Our records code the county's grounds that LUBA reviewed (plus some LUBA grounds for Jackson) and have no raised-by or ruling fields. (3) The design assumes county approvals are loaded or "pending"; real county approvals are not loaded, so the real map is shown in the pending state. (4) The LUBA explainer's "usually within 21 days" and the affirm/reverse/remand outcomes are unverified general knowledge and must be checked against LUBA's own pages before the site states them.
- **Limitations / open questions:** screen-reader testing has not been done. Contrast was checked by formula, not by people using the site.

### Website built to the design
- **Decision or analysis:** `site/index.html`, `site/js/` and the design stylesheets form the public site. Data reaches the page only through `site/data/site-data.json` and the county layer; pure helpers (`site/js/lib.js`: map projection, count classes, data state, keyboard moves, outcome labels) are tested with Node (`tests/js`), and `tests/test_site_page.py` checks the page structure and safety rules.
- **Choices where the design and our data differed:** the appeal examples show the real county outcomes (two denials, one conditional approval) and our reasons, not petitioner assignments of error (we do not code those). Reasons under a LUBA case are grouped as "given for the county decision" (solid) and "given in the appeal decision" (hatched). Quotes are shown with the document and PDF page (`SHOW_QUOTES` in `site/js/config.js`). Reviewer notes are not shown. Real county approvals are not loaded, so real mode shows the design's "not loaded yet" state; no zero is shown where data is missing. Synthetic mode shows the synthetic county approvals only and says the appeal examples are not shown with synthetic data.
- **Safeguards:** all text from data is set with `textContent` (a test forbids `innerHTML` and similar), scripts use relative URLs only, nothing is loaded from other servers, the word "final" appears only in the "not final" notice, and the synthetic banner, tag and title prefix apply in synthetic mode. The data-mode choice is kept in the address (`?data=`).
- **Checked by hand:** in headless Chromium the page loaded with no console errors and no requests outside the site, had no horizontal scroll at 390 px width, selecting a county by link or arrow key updated the panel, and a case opened from its hash.
- **Limitations / open questions:** `site-data.json` holds both the real and synthetic sections in one file, so the page downloads both (the design asked for loading synthetic data only when chosen); the page never shows the two together. The browser behaviour was checked by a one-off script, not a committed test. Screen-reader testing has not been done. See `docs/site-content.md` for wording that must be verified before launch.

- **What was confirmed:** an appeal must be filed within 21 days after the decision becomes final (generally when put in writing); local appeal steps must finish first; the petitioner is whoever appeals and the local government is the respondent; LUBA works from the county's record, takes no new evidence in the usual case, and holds one hearing for legal argument; LUBA's orders end in affirm, reverse, remand, dismiss or transfer; a LUBA order can be appealed to the Court of Appeals within 21 days of mailing; an appeal does not automatically stop an approved project. **Reverse** is required when the governing body exceeded its jurisdiction, the decision is unconstitutional, or it violates applicable law and is prohibited as a matter of law. **Remand** is required when findings are insufficient, the decision lacks substantial evidence, procedural errors prejudice the petitioner's substantial rights, the law was misconstrued but the decision is not prohibited, or all parties stipulate.
- **Effect on the coding rules:** none needed. The repo's earlier wording (reversal when prohibited as a matter of law) was right. LUBA can overturn only on the legal grounds the statute lists, so "valid is not decisive" (Yamhill) fits the substantial-evidence standard: the county may choose which evidence to believe, and an alternative sufficient ground can carry the result.
- **Limitations / open questions:** the appeal outcome list has no "transferred" value (LUBA transfers an appeal to circuit court when the decision is not a land use decision); no sample case needs it. The standing rule for petitioners is not stated in the FAQ and is not stated on the site. The FAQ page's date is unknown, so re-check it before launch.

### Measuring the extraction against hand-coded reasons (`ofs compare`)
- **Decision or analysis:** a repeatable comparison of the model's proposed quotes with the hand-coded reasons for the same document, page by page.
- **Why (and alternatives rejected):** the extraction is only useful if its misses and extras are known before anyone relies on it. Page-level matching on quote overlap was chosen because the category is not assigned by the model, so only the quotes can be compared. Exact-string matching was rejected: the model and the hand coder may stop at different words of the same sentence.
- **Inputs:** `data/records/*.json` (hand-coded reasons, `hand_checked`), `<doc-id>.candidates.json` and `<doc-id>.manifest.json` from `ofs extract` (git-ignored, local).
- **Assumptions and definitions:** a match is a proposed and a hand-coded quote on the same PDF page whose in-order word overlap is at least 50% of the shorter quote ("same" if at least 90% overlap and lengths within 20%, otherwise "partial"). Each quote is used once, best overlap first. Recall counts only hand-coded reasons on pages the model read (the manifest lists them). A miss notes whether the quote check held the model's quote back for review or rejected it, because that points to the gate and not the model.
- **Limitations / open questions:** the hand-coded set is selective (one quote per distinct reason; for example Yamhill's overall "on balance" paragraph is not coded), so extras are expected and are not counted as errors. A person must triage them. Overlap says nothing about whether the model's reading of *why* the county or LUBA decided is right. Three documents is a small sample, and the hand-coded reasons are the same ones used to tune the prompt, so recall here is optimistic.

### First full comparison of the extraction against hand-coded reasons (2026-10-21)
- **Decision or analysis:** ran `ofs extract` over every page of the five documents that have hand-coded reasons and compared the result with them using `ofs compare`.
- **Inputs:** the three LUBA opinions and the Marion staff report listed in `docs/data-sources.md` (checksums are in each local run manifest); `data/records/*.json`. Model `qwen3:30b-instruct` (digest begins `19e422b02313`), temperature 0, seed 42, prompt checksum begins `6ff713f6d0dd`, pipeline commit begins `25c7daed`. Reports and manifests stay in git-ignored `data/local/`.
- **Result (counts):** 13 of 16 hand-coded reasons found on the pages scanned (5 near-identical, 8 partial overlap); 3 missed; 195 proposed quotes matched no hand-coded reason. Across the five documents the model accepted 208 quotes, held 11 for review, rejected 14 and lost 1 page to an error. By document: Yamhill 5 of 6 (86 extras), Marion LUBA opinion 3 of 4 (38), Marion staff report 1 of 1 (6), Jackson 2017 3 of 4 (58), Jackson 2019 1 of 1 (7).
- **The 3 misses:** one hand-coded quote (Yamhill p41) was rejected by the quote check as over 40 words; two (Marion p7, Jackson p12) were not proposed, though the model proposed other sentences from the same passages on those pages.
- **The page error:** on Marion p10 the model labeled a quote "petitioner", which the parser treated as malformed and so discarded the whole page. Fixed in a follow-up change: an unexpected label now rejects only that quote.
- **Limitations / open questions:** the 195 extras have not been reviewed, so precision is unknown. A sample shows a mix: county findings the hand coder did not pick (the hand set is selective), LUBA statements of the review standard or procedure, and fragments of the petitioner's argument. The hand-coded reasons were used to tune the prompt, so 13 of 16 is optimistic for unseen documents. A match compares quotes only, not categories. The per-page yes/no answer is not saved in the output, so a "not proposed" miss cannot show whether the model said the page had no reasons or chose other sentences.
